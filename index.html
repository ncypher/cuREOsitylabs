QuickBase & ServiceNow

A powerful foundation, turned into software people enjoy using.

QuickBase is a strong engine with a dated dashboard. We build modern custom software on top of it: full applications that run natively inside QuickBase code pages, plus middleware that connects it to QuickBooks and the rest of your tools. Same engine, but it drives like a sports car instead of a bulldozer.

  • Custom apps inside QuickBase code pages
  • Modern, phone-friendly interfaces
  • QuickBooks integrations
  • Middleware connectors
  • ServiceNow applications
  • Cleanup of existing builds

A typical first jobPut a clean, phone-friendly app on top of an existing QuickBase setup, so the field crew taps through their jobs instead of fighting forms, and invoices flow into QuickBooks on their own.

Sensors & LoRa mesh

Custom IoT that works with or without the internet.

We write custom IoT software for sensors and devices that keeps running whether or not the internet is up. Devices talk to each other over a local LoRa radio mesh, keep their readings on-site, and sync to the cloud only when you want them to.

  • Works offline or online
  • Custom IoT software
  • Temperature and equipment sensors
  • LoRa mesh with Meshtastic
  • ESP32 hardware
  • Enclosures built by hand in REO Town

A typical first jobPut a sensor in a walk-in cooler and raise an alert when it drifts, over a long-range radio link with no monthly cell bill.

Field notes

The ideas the workshop runs on, written down. Build logs from real projects will land here as they ship.

October 14, 2026

Software as a local trade

Ransom E. Olds founded the REO Motor Car Company in Lansing in 1904, after leaving the company that carried his name, to build tough, dependable working machines like the REO Speed Wagon on his own terms.

CuREOsity Labs puts REO inside curiosity. It treats software, databases, radio, and AI the way a neighborhood machine shop treats steel: as material for custom tools, made by people you can meet.

October 14, 2026

Where the currents meet

Near the north edge of REO Town, the Red Cedar curves in to join the Grand. Two currents become one stronger river. The workshop sits at a similar meeting point, between local know-how and modern data.

At a river bend, an eddy is water that runs back against the main flow. In a conductor, an eddy current is a loop of energy induced by a changing field. We aim to be both: a counter-current to tech monopolies that still generates real power, right here.

October 14, 2026

Bring the fire down to earth

Small businesses pay rent every month on bloated software they don’t control. The capabilities behind it, AI agents, integrations, custom databases, and automation, aren’t magic. They can be built smaller, cheaper, and closer to home.

The goal is leverage: let the local shop, the regional contractor, and the independent underdog work with the same tools as a national chain, without giving up ownership of their data or the way they like to work.

REO Town has always built things

This neighborhood grew up around a factory that put working machines in ordinary hands. The plant is gone, but the habit stuck: REO Town is still a place where people make, fix, and figure things out.

CuREOsity Labs carries that forward with software. Founded October 13, 2026, it’s a workshop for custom databases, web applications, integrations, sensors, and AI, sized for the businesses that keep this city running.
